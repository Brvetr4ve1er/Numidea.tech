## 1. What this campaign is and realistic expectations

### What it is
This is a small, time-boxed **learning test**. People in France and the Gulf see an ad and tap it. WhatsApp then opens on `{{WA}}` with a message already typed. That message includes a **ref code** showing which ad they saw. You qualify them in the chat. If they qualify, you offer an Aperçu, build it, and present it on a 30-minute video call.

We use WhatsApp rather than a form for two reasons:
- **There is no privacy URL yet**, so Meta instant lead forms are not used. They stay off for the whole test, even after the privacy page goes live before launch (§2 B).
- WhatsApp Business policy allows conversations that **the person starts**, which is exactly what a click-to-WhatsApp ad produces. It does not allow cold messaging.

### Realistic expectations
- Expect **0 to 3 qualified prospects in total** from the ~USD 125. Zero is a possible outcome.
- **Do not expect signed contracts during the test week.** Qualified leads have up to 3 months to decide, so you will only know the revenue result by about **mid-February 2027**.
- This is a **learning budget**. Money left unspent because a stop rule fired counts as savings, not failure.

### What the test should teach you
1. Which market responds better: France or the Gulf.
2. Which concept gets taps and qualified chats:
   - (1) "Voyez votre nouveau site avant de payer" / see it before you pay
   - (2) the work carousel: 2 client sites + 1 portfolio demo
   - (3) the price anchor
3. Why chats fail to qualify: no SIRET or trade licence/CR, a budget below the Essentiel floor, or no decision timeline.
4. Whether you can reply within the working day and deliver an Aperçu in 5 working days alongside your other work.

**What success looks like for this test:** at least one qualified conversation, at least one Aperçu booked, and clean per-ref data to support a go/no-go decision (§8).

### Creative rules (apply to every ad)
- **Advantage+ creative enhancements: off.** That means no automatic text variations, no auto-translation and no generated backgrounds. This keeps France French-only and every claim exactly as written.
- **Conditions:** every ad carries the conditions line above. Wherever the 5 working days appear in prospect-facing text, add the condition: « à réception de vos contenus » / "once we have your content" / «من استلام المحتوى».
- **Prices:** every price is marked approximate and excluding tax. In French copy, write « environ » or « env. » with « HT ». Use "~" only when space is short, and keep « HT » in every case. ### 6.6 Contract: itemised copyright transfer (French CPI L131-2 / L131-3)
For French clients, write the contract, including this clause, in French, or bilingual with French first.

Under French law, a copyright transfer must be **in writing** (L131-2). **Each right transferred must be mentioned separately**, and its scope must be defined by **extent, purpose, place and duration** (L131-3). A vague "all rights transferred" clause is not enough. The contract should list:

- **Rights transferred:** reproduction (copy, store, host), representation (publish online, communicate to the public), adaptation and modification, and translation (multilingual sites).
- **Purpose and media:** for example, the client's website and related digital channels.
- **Territory:** for example, worldwide.
- **Duration:** for example, the full legal term of protection.
- **Exclusive or non-exclusive.**
- **Price:** included in the project price, or stated separately.
- **Timing:** the transfer takes effect **on full payment**.
- **Exclusions:**
  - third-party components (open-source libraries, fonts, stock images) stay under their own licences;
  - the client's own content remains theirs;
  - your pre-existing generic code and tools are **licensed**, not transferred.
- **Portfolio clause:** whether you may show the project in your portfolio. Ask; do not assume.

Have the lawyer review the template once before the first contract.
